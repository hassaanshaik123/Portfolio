01Selected Work · AI ProductBUILDING · 2026

Raah.AI —
career diagnosis,
not career advice.

An AI platform that assesses where a student actually stands — education, interests, aptitude — then narrows the world of work to 4 industries and 4 roles, drops them into a real work simulation, and hands them a personalized growth report.

ProblemCareer advice is generic, late, anecdotal
RoleFounder & product lead
OutputDiagnosis → simulation → report flow
StageBuilding · piloting 2026

01The problem

Most students choose careers the way they choose a Netflix show: on vibes. Internet advice, peer pressure, one-off counseling sessions — all opinion, no assessment. The cost doesn't show up at 18. It shows up at 25, as disengagement, pivots, and reskilling debt.

I've mentored enough founders-in-the-making to see the pattern: the bottleneck is never ambition. It's diagnosis. Nobody runs an assessment before prescribing a path.

02The product flow

Raah.AI treats career choice like a medical diagnosis: assess → narrow → simulate → report. Each stage cuts the option space while raising confidence:

  1. Diagnose
  2. 4 Industries
  3. 4 Roles
  4. Simulation
  5. Report

The simulation is the moat. Resumes say what you did; simulations show how you think. A student who believes they want "marketing" discovers in 20 minutes whether they actually enjoy positioning trade-offs — or just the idea of it.

03What I'm building next

  • Pilot cohort — running the full flow with a first cohort of Masters' Union students and comparing outcomes to their stated intentions.
  • Eval harness — measuring recommendation quality: precision of the 4-role shortlist against post-simulation self-assessment.
  • B2B2C channel — placement cells and college E-Cells as distribution, because institutions own the moment of career anxiety.

04Why this is the through-line

Every venture I've run follows the same shape: diagnose the real bottleneck, build the system, measure the outcome. Hustlers' Edge diagnosed the intention-to-income gap. NexClip diagnosed why campus content dies. Raah.AI applies the same operating system to the most expensive decision a student makes.
