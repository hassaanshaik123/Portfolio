NexClip Media —
19M views,
zero ad spend.

A campus media venture that grew 13K+ followers and 19M+ organic views by treating content like a product: formats were experiments, the audience was the metric, and distribution was designed, not hoped for.

ProblemCampus content dies with each batch
RoleFounder & growth lead
OutputRepeatable format system
Evidence19M+ views · 0 paid spend

01The problem

Campus pages are notoriously spiky and disposable — one viral meme, then silence, then the next batch starts from zero. Nobody treats the page itself as a product with retention, formats, and a growth loop. The audience was there; the operating system wasn't.

NexClip was my attempt to prove that attention is a system, not luck — and it became the cheapest product-management education I could have bought.

02The growth system

We ran the page like an experiment pipeline: ship weekly formats → measure watch-through and shares → kill the bottom half → double down on winners. Formats, not one-off posts, were the unit of production:

  • Format over post — recurring series (campus confessions, spotlight interviews, meme formats) that the audience learned to expect and tag friends into.
  • Distribution by design — every format had a built-in share trigger; growth came from the audience doing the distribution.
  • Kill fast, scale fast — formats got 2–3 weeks of evidence; underperformers were retired without sentimentality.

The compounding is the point: the curve stayed flat for months, then bent — because formats accumulate an audience that formats before them built.

04What it taught me

Before NexClip I thought growth was a creativity problem. It's actually an evidence problem: volume of experiments × speed of iteration × honesty about what died. That mental model — ship, measure, kill, double down — is the same one I now bring to product decisions at Raah.AI, just with a funnel dashboard instead of a meme calendar.
